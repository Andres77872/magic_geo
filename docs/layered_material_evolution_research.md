# Layered material evolution: events, remapping and thermal continuation

Reviewed 2026-09-11. This work implements fixed finite material transfers and homogeneous layer remapping on the previously qualified layered ice graph. Both component suites pass independent exact-arithmetic replay. Production world generation still uses the installed native library; these internal components have not been connected to the state owner or annual simulation.

## Physical interpretation and dependencies

Cold ice must carry its cold content when it moves into a surface layer. Liquid carries latent and, when present, sensible heat. The recipient's complete enthalpy determines refreezing after mixing; adding a separate latent-heat release would count the same energy twice.

CTSM's snow hydrology makes retention, receiving capacity and downward process order explicit, with bottom drainage passed to runoff and infiltration. That motivates a distinct transport policy rather than inferring drainage from a thermodynamic update. Our finite batch samples all donors initially; a sequential downward drainage policy would need successive certified batches. [CTSM snow hydrology, sections 2.8.3 and 2.8.7](https://escomp.github.io/CTSM/tech_note/Snow_Hydrology/CLM50_Tech_Note_Snow_Hydrology.html)

Verjans et al. distinguish bucket, matrix and preferential flow in firn, including refreezing limited by cold content. Preferential flow can bypass cold material, so complete mixing at each receiving layer remains a declared simplification. This work adopts no hydraulic threshold or calibration from that comparison. [Verjans et al., 2019](https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/13/1819/2019/)

Crocus describes conserving mass and enthalpy when merging and splitting layers, with merged density derived from combined mass and thickness. Our remap conserves ideal water mass and enthalpy but accepts explicitly prescribed replacement density and conductivity. Volume conservation therefore requires an additional compatible density choice. Splitting a homogeneous layer does not reconstruct unresolved temperature gradients. [Crocus 3.0.2, sections 2.3.2–2.3.3](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/19/6273/2026/)

The new dependency chain is explicit: validate the source graph and its uncertainty domain → certify initial donor availability → transfer signed mass/enthalpy parcels → rebuild the material graph → remap water and stationary nonwater energy → rebuild geometry and conductance → advance the actual resulting thermal request. A refusal stops dependent work. No later heating or incoming transfer can repair an invalid earlier operation within the same batch.

## Implemented contracts

[Material events](../cpp/src/engine/layered_ice_material.hpp) address each active layer or positive deep inventory by geographic cell and layer. Internal transfers remain within one column. Imports, exports and transfers have unique batch-local IDs; the wrapper retains one shared carried energy for each internal debit and credit. An explicit pure-water calorimeter entry supports zero nonwater capacity while preserving the old entry's positive-capacity admission rule. A disabled or unsupported calorimeter returns typed unavailability before starting a calorimeter call.

All gross outgoing phase demands use the initial inventory. In extensive units, with mass `M=A W`, energy `Q=A H`, latent heat `L` and inherited global energy radius `E`, positive liquid demand `m` requires `m<=M` and `m L<=Q−E`; positive solid demand requires `m<=M` and `Q+E<=(M−m)L`. Each node receives the full marginal radius `E/A`, rather than an unproved share divided by node count. Empty pure-water thermal outputs refuse; complete layer removal belongs to a topology transaction.

[Remapping](../cpp/src/engine/layered_ice_remap.hpp) requires every positive-water donor exactly once and normalizes positive integer weights by their exact integer sum. Water mass and water enthalpy follow the same fractions. Top nonwater capacity and its energy remain at the geographic top. For top capacity `C`, its stationary energy is `C(T−Tf)`; the water part is the remaining complete enthalpy. Pure-water and dry-top identities avoid unnecessary subtraction. Output thickness and conductance are rebuilt from the resulting mass and prescribed material descriptors. Target density changes are explicit geometry changes, including for a state-identity remap.

The independent [physical derivation](../runs/layered-material-evolution-review/evidence/layered-material-physical-review/physical-review.md) establishes nonexpansion of each fixed operation in the extensive sum of absolute energy errors. Donor sensitivity partitions between retained material, recipients and new external outboxes; the coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one. Remapping similarly partitions water and stationary nonwater energy. The inherited global error is consequently charged once. Add the direct represented-versus-ideal retained energy defect and, for material exports, the new outbox defect. Signed global cancellation alone is insufficient to bound local energy error.

## Remaining production work

The energy bound is conditional on each declared canonical, rounded water mass. Receipts expose mass projection intervals, but do not propagate those mass differences through later temperature, phase availability, thickness or conductance. An original-material trajectory needs either exact extensive mass ownership plus certified interface conversion, or a separately propagated mass-error bound with its thermal and geometric effects. Joules and kilograms cannot be added into one error allowance.

The current event wrapper requires a valid positive-storage intermediate graph. Full ablation followed by layer removal or deep refill may therefore need one joint material/remap transaction rather than separately committed calls. No epsilon mass is inserted. Empty deep stores are retained only under their exact empty constraint and are not existing event receivers.

The next owner must map geographic cells to layers, retain deep material and unresolved outboxes, bind event replay identities across transactions, and atomically commit the graph, thermal state, accumulated bounds and ledgers. Its SDIRK2 flux ledger needs the method's stage weights. Routing delivery and acknowledgment must account for each outbox exactly once. The reviewed [owner contract](../runs/layered-material-evolution-review/evidence/layered-material-successor-final-review/owner-contract.md) records these next-step requirements without claiming implementation. A physical source/drainage/basal policy, layer initialization, geometry readiness before descendants, versioned downstream regeneration and a newly designed production-year qualification remain open. The closed historical annual and six-hour experiments were not rerun.

This work changes no viewer or API behavior. Earlier adopted dependency, availability and request-order corrections remain described in [current simulation status](current_simulation_review_status.md). The [portable evidence package](../runs/layered-material-evolution-review/README.md) binds source versions, inputs, first failures, final results and independent readers; its verifier replays retained data without native simulation calls.
